The Colloquy of Mobiles

A conversation between five machines

Five bodies hang from a rack. Three of them — the females — turn slowly on their armatures, each carrying a mirror and a hunger for light. Two more — the males — ride a rotating beam above the plinth, sweeping the room with a lamp, looking for something to answer them.

Neither can satisfy itself alone. When a male's beam finds a female's mirror and the reflection comes back, the two lock on and trade signals until both are spent. Then they drift apart and begin again.

What this site shows is not an animation of that idea. It is a recording of the simulation itself — two minutes of it, unedited, every tick a complete state of every body — played back under your scroll.

It runs a whole cycle. The five begin spent, then fall to searching, and for most of the scroll nothing finds anything. Two thirds of the way down Female A and Male I catch each other and hold — and then she lets go, he does, and all five go back to hunting.

The Colloquy of Mobiles

Built three times

  1. 1968

    Cybernetic Serendipity

    Institute of Contemporary Arts, London

    Gordon Pask built the Colloquy for the exhibition Jasia Reichardt opened at the ICA in August 1968 — the show that put computers, cybernetics and art in one room for the first time in Britain.

    Five mobiles hung from a rack over a plinth, talking to each other in light and sound. Visitors worked out that they could join in: a pocket mirror or a torch was enough to be taken for one of them.

  2. Fifty years

  3. 2018

    Built again as hardware

    Fiftieth anniversary reconstruction

    Fifty years on, the piece was made a second time as a physical thing — the same five bodies, the same appetites, the same conversation carried on light and sound.

    The original survives mostly as photographs, a handful of accounts and Pask's own description of how it was meant to behave. A reconstruction has to actually run, so every gap in that record becomes a decision somebody has to make and defend.

  4. Eight years

  5. 2026

    The digital recreation

    Colloquy Scene Studio

    The bodies are modelled from measured drawings, and the behaviour is a simulation of Pask's logic rather than an animation of it: drives that grow, a search, an encounter, satisfaction, and a return to searching.

    Nothing is choreographed. The studio runs the five of them and records what they do — and what this site scrubs under your scroll is one of those recordings, two minutes of it, unedited.
